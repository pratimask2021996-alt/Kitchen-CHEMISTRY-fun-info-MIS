First, a fact

Searing a steak does not "lock in" its juices — that's a kitchen myth. The crust forms because high, dry heat drives the Maillard reaction between amino acids and sugars. It has nothing to do with moisture, and everything to do with chemistry.

Why your kitchen counts as a lab

Cooking is applied chemistry with better smells. Heat unfolds proteins and rearranges sugars. Acids and bases swap electrons and release gas. Microbes you can't see convert flour and milk into bread and cheese. Nothing on this site requires special equipment — it's already sitting in your cupboard.
